# Operation Before the Storm
## Echoes of the Great Patriotic War

*In the style of Genshin Impact's "Moonward Finale Act IV: Gazing Upon the Vanished Moonlight"*
*SoMe Universe — Year 2343, Campaign Headquarters, Lymph Node Citadel*

---

## Prologue: The Waiting Hour

The tactical table in the Lymph Node war room was a slab of obsidian twenty meters long, embedded with a three-dimensional holographic projection system that could render the entire Continent Sacrum in miniature. Right now, it showed the region known as the **Cervical Plateau**—a fortress of immune infrastructure that the Umin had renamed **Phoenix** three years ago, after the symbol they had burned into its walls.

Three years of occupation. Three years of silence from the Alopesian underground.

And now, finally, a counteroffensive was being planned.

Lieutenant **Roi Hytar** stood at the eastern end of the table, her fingers tracing the defensive perimeters that Umin forces had constructed around the fortress. She had been awake for thirty-one hours. Her uniform jacket bore the ragged edges of a recent engagement near the **Thymus Corridor**—the last supply route before the Cervical Plateau—and the burn mark on her pauldron was not decoration.

She did not look tired. She looked like someone who had learned to function on spite alone.

Behind her, the war room door hissed open. **Colonel Sable**, commander of the Third Mobile Regiment, entered first, followed by **Ambassador Lian** of the Eastern Therapeutics Coalition, **Dr. Kael** of the Preceptor Order, and two figures whose presence was, to put it mildly, contentious.

The first was **Nightingale**—not a person, but a Helicarrier-class vessel that had somehow manifested in human form, her philosophy of *"preserve the force you have"* having become legendary after the Siege of the Lymph Node. The second was **Drift**, whose allegiances were still a matter of heated debate in Alopesian intelligence circles.

Sable stopped at the table and planted his fists on its edge. "Are we ready to talk about the actual operation, or are we going to spend another hour discussing *philosophy*?"

---

## Act I: "All Have Spoken"

### Scene 1: The Briefing

Colonel Sable activated the central hologram. The view zoomed in on Phoenix—a massive structure of bone-white walls and arterial-red corridors, perched at the junction of four major highways. Umin banners hung from its parapets, stitched with the symbol of the Uncrowned: a crown of thorns, rendered in purple thread.

"Three years ago, the Umin took Phoenix in seventy-two hours," Sable said. His voice was flat, the voice of a man who had practiced this briefing until it hurt. "They did it by cutting the **Subclavian Supply Line**, isolating the garrison, and then flooding the inner courtyard with **Silencing Fog**—a chemical agent that disrupts immune cell communication. The garrison didn't fall in combat. It fell in *silence*."

He tapped the hologram, and the image shifted to show a network of red lines radiating from Phoenix like a spider's web.

"Current Umin garrison: estimated twelve thousand combatants, including **Voice Knights** and at least two **Silken Baron** commanders. Defensive infrastructure includes the Silencing Fog network, a redesigned perimeter with automated response turrets, and—" He paused. "And a presence we have not fully identified. Preceptor intelligence suggests a **Harbinger-class operative** is embedded in the command structure."

Dr. Kael stepped forward. "My order has designated this operative as **Designation: Mute**. Their specialty is counter-intelligence. They have been systematically dismantling resistance cells from within."

Sable nodded grimly. "Which is why our infiltration phase has already suffered three compromises. We are operating with reduced operational security."

---

### Scene 2: The Four Pillars

Ambassador Lian spoke next, her voice carrying the measured cadence of someone who had negotiated too many treaties to trust simple solutions.

"The Eastern Therapeutics Coalition has committed three combat brigades to the operation: the **First Bone Remission**, the **Serum Shock Battalion**, and the **Adjuvant Support Group**. Combined with Colonel Sable's Third Mobile Regiment and Lieutenant Hytar's special operations unit, our total combat strength is approximately four thousand."

The room absorbed this number.

"Four thousand against twelve thousand," Roi Hytar said flatly. "And fortified."

"Correct," Lian replied. "Which is why this cannot be a frontal assault. We need a **four-phase operation** that achieves surprise at multiple points simultaneously."

She activated a new display—a tactical diagram showing Phoenix from above, with four colored arrows pointing toward it from different directions.

"**Phase One: The Subclavian Cut.** Three years ago, the Umin cut our supply line to take Phoenix. We will do the same to them. Colonel Sable's Third Mobile Regiment will advance through the **Thymus Corridor**, bypass the main Umin defensive positions, and seize the **Junction of Four Arteries**—the chokepoint where Phoenix receives 70% of its supplies and reinforcements. Once this junction is taken, Phoenix's garrison will have fourteen days of supplies before they begin to starve. This is not an attack. This is a *siege*."

Sable studied the route on the hologram. "The Thymus Corridor is held by Umin **Pathogen Sentry Units**—light infantry, but they have sensor networks embedded in the tissue walls. We'll need electronic warfare support to pass unnoticed."

"Which brings me to Phase Two," Lian continued.

---

### Scene 3: The Electronic War

Dr. Kael raised his hand, and the hologram shifted to show a different angle—a cross-section of Phoenix's internal structure, revealing the Silencing Fog network that the Umin had installed in every corridor.

"Phase Two: **Operation Quiet Thunder.** The Umin's Silencing Fog is not merely a defensive weapon. It is a *communications system*—a network that allows Voice Knights to coordinate across the entire fortress without using conventional radio frequencies, which would be jammed. If we can disrupt this network, their garrison will be forced to communicate the old-fashioned way: runners, signals, line-of-sight."

"And how do we disrupt it?" Sable asked.

Dr. Kael turned to Nightingale. "That is her area of expertise."

Nightingale stepped forward, and her voice filled the room—that same quality of distant bells, calm and resonant.

"The Silencing Fog network has a vulnerability. It requires *maintenance nodes*—small devices scattered throughout the fortress that refresh the chemical agents and calibrate the broadcast range. These nodes are not defended, because the Umin believe the fog itself protects them. But I have been analyzing the network for three years, and I believe I can guide a team to **seven key nodes** before the garrison realizes what is happening."

The hologram marked seven points inside Phoenix's walls.

"If all seven nodes are neutralized within a six-hour window, the Silencing Fog network will collapse. The Voice Knights will lose their coordination advantage, and their response to our assault will be uncoordinated."

Roi Hytar studied the marked positions. "Seven points. That's a lot of deep insertion."

"It is," Nightingale agreed. "Which is why I will lead one team personally. But I need six others—special operations personnel capable of moving through hostile territory without detection."

Drift spoke for the first time, their voice soft but carrying. "I know the internal layout of Phoenix. I know where the patrol routes are. I can guide one team."

The room turned to look at Drift. The tension was palpable.

Sable's jaw tightened. "And why should we trust you?"

---

### Scene 4: The Question of Trust

Drift did not flinch.

"Because I was there," they said quietly, "when the Umin took Phoenix. Not as a defender. As a... *witness*."

The room went still.

"I was a researcher at the **Cervical Institute of Immunity** when the Umin arrived. I watched the garrison fall. I watched the Silencing Fog roll through the streets, and I watched the Voice Knights drag my colleagues to the interrogation chambers." Drift's voice did not waver. "I survived because I was useful. I know Phoenix's infrastructure better than anyone else in this room—better than most Umin officers. I know where they made changes during the occupation, and where they did not. I know which corridors they trust and which ones they have forgotten."

"And you expect us to believe you've had a change of heart?" Sable's voice was ice.

"I expect nothing," Drift replied. "I am offering information. Whether you use it is your decision. But if you assault Phoenix without my knowledge, you will lose people unnecessarily. The Umin have changed the drainage system in the **Eastern Wing**. They have collapsed the maintenance tunnels beneath the **Courtyard of Bones**. They have booby-trapped the **Arterial Bridge** that your intelligence documents still show as a viable approach."

Roi Hytar studied Drift's face. Something in those eyes—something that looked like grief, or guilt, or both.

"I'll vouch for them," she said.

The room turned again.

"They guided our reconnaissance unit through the **Peripheral Zones** six months ago," Hytar continued. "They could have turned us in a dozen times. They didn't. That's not nothing."

Sable's expression was unreadable. "This is not a democracy, Lieutenant."

"No," Hytar agreed. "But you asked why we should trust them. I'm giving you a reason. Whether it's enough is your call, sir."

A long silence.

Finally, Colonel Sable exhaled. "Fine. Drift is attached to Nightingale's team. But if this is a trap—if we are compromised through this arrangement—it will be the last decision you ever make, Drift. Understood?"

Drift nodded once. "Understood."

---

## Act II: "The Shape of the Plan"

### Scene 5: Phase Three — The Bone Marrow Gambit

Ambassador Lian activated the next phase of the tactical display.

"Phase Three: **Operation Bone Marrow**. Once the Subclavian Cut has been established and the Silencing Fog network has collapsed, we will need a *visible* force to draw the Umin's attention. Colonel Sable's Third Mobile Regiment will advance on Phoenix from the northeast—not to assault the walls, but to *threaten* them. The Umin will be forced to respond with their reserves, which will thin the garrison in other sectors."

Sable studied the approach route. "Northeast approach means crossing the **Calcified Flats**. Open terrain. My tanks will be exposed."

"For approximately four kilometers," Lian agreed. "After that, the terrain becomes urban. You will have cover."

"Four kilometers is a long time to be shot at."

"You will have air support. **Nightingale's escort wing** will provide overhead cover until you reach the urban corridor. After that—" She glanced at Nightingale. "After that, Nightingale returns to the deep insertion teams."

Nightingale nodded. "My vessel cannot enter the urban environment without risking civilian casualties. The escort wing will stay with Colonel Sable's advance. I will continue the node neutralization personally."

Dr. Kael raised a concern. "What about the Harbinger operative? Mute? If they realize the Subclavian Cut is happening, they will redirect reserves to the Junction of Four Arteries. Our siege timeline depends on that junction holding."

Lian smiled thinly. "Which brings us to Phase Four."

---

### Scene 6: The Hidden Knife

The hologram shifted to show the interior of Phoenix—not the corridors and courtyards visible to occupiers, but the *secret* architecture that only exist in maintenance schematics and whispered rumors.

"Phase Four: **Operation Quietus**. While Colonel Sable's regiment threatens from the northeast, while Nightingale's teams neutralize the Silencing Fog nodes, and while Dr. Kael's Preceptor operatives feed disinformation to the garrison—Lieutenant Hytar's special operations unit will execute a **direct extraction**."

The hologram zoomed in on a specific location: a tower in the center of Phoenix, marked with the designation **COMMAND SPIRE**.

"Mute—the Harbinger operative—is the garrison's counter-intelligence asset. They are the reason our resistance cells keep getting compromised. But Mute is also the reason the garrison's command structure is *dependent* on them. The Silken Barons who command Phoenix do not trust each other. They trust Mute to keep them informed. If Mute is removed—or, more specifically, if Mute's network of informants is *disrupted*—the command structure will fracture."

Roi Hytar studied the Command Spire. "How do we disrupt Mute's network?"

"We do not need to destroy it. We need to *expose* it. Dr. Kael has identified seventeen suspected informants embedded in the Phoenix garrison—Alopesian civilians who have been turned, threatened, or bribed into feeding information to Mute. If these informants receive a *pre-arranged signal* at the moment of our assault, they will believe their cover is blown. They will panic. They will either flee or go silent. Either way, Mute will lose their eyes and ears at the worst possible moment."

"And in that window, my unit takes the Spire," Hytar said.

"Correct. Not to hold it. To *broadcast*. We will use the Spire's communications array to transmit a message to every Umin soldier in Phoenix: not a surrender demand, but a *question*. 'What are you fighting for?' The Silencing Fog will be down. The supply lines will be cut. The Visible threat will be pressing from outside. And then, from inside, they will hear a voice asking them to *think*."

Dr. Kael spoke quietly. "It will not make all of them surrender."

"No," Lian agreed. "But it will make *some* of them hesitate. And in combat, hesitation is often enough."

---

### Scene 7: The Contingencies

Colonel Sable pulled up the contingency protocols on the side display.

"Every operation needs a failure mode. Here is ours."

The display showed a branching diagram of possible complications:

**If the Subclavian Cut fails:**
- Sable's regiment will establish a defensive position at the Junction of Four Arteries and hold for seventy-two hours while the command reassesses.
- The Eastern Therapeutics brigades will fall back to the **Lymph Node Citadel** and prepare for a prolonged siege.
- Phoenix will not be liberated in this campaign.

**If the Silencing Fog nodes cannot be neutralized:**
- Nightingale will abort the deep insertion and concentrate on supporting Sable's advance.
- The assault will proceed without electronic superiority, with expected casualties increasing by approximately forty percent.
- The operation will become a grinding siege rather than a coordinated strike.

**If the Harbinger operative—Mute—identifies the infiltration before Phase Four:**
- Dr. Kael will activate a **Preceptor Distress Protocol**, flooding the Phoenix garrison with false intelligence.
- The false intelligence will indicate that the main assault is coming from the *southwest*, forcing Mute to redirect assets away from the actual breakthrough point.
- This will buy Hytar's unit an estimated thirty-minute window before Mute corrects their analysis.

**If Lieutenant Hytar's unit is compromised inside the Command Spire:**
- Nightingale will deploy a **Surgical Strike Package**—a targeted bombardment designed to destroy the Spire's communication array without structural collapse.
- This will deny Phoenix the ability to coordinate external reinforcements, even at the cost of Hytar's unit.
- Dr. Kael will posthumously designate Hytar's team as **Protocol Martyrs**, inspiring further resistance operations.

The room read through the contingencies in silence.

Finally, Roi Hytar spoke. "I want to add a fifth contingency."

Sable raised an eyebrow. "Which is?"

"If I personally am compromised—if I am captured or killed before the broadcast—the broadcast must still happen. It does not require my voice. It requires *a* voice. Any member of my unit can complete the mission. Anyone can ask the question. The question does not need me."

She met Sable's eyes. "The mission is not about me. It is about the question."

---

## Act III: "The Choice Point"

### Scene 8: The Final Assembly

The briefing ended at midnight.

Most of the participants dispersed to their quarters or their duties. But Roi Hytar lingered, standing before the holographic map, watching the red pulses that marked Umin positions around Phoenix.

Dr. Kael approached. "You should rest."

"I could say the same to you."

Kael smiled faintly. "Preceptors do not rest. We watch. We wait. We... calculate."

"Calculate what?"

"The probability of success. The cost of failure. The number of people who will not come home." Kael's voice was soft. "I have been calculating since the briefing began. The numbers are... not kind."

Hytar did not look at him. "What are the numbers?"

"Four thousand deployed. Approximately six hundred will not return. That is a fifteen percent casualty rate—within acceptable parameters for an operation of this scale. But the numbers do not tell you which six hundred. They do not tell you which ones will die thinking they could have done more, or which ones will die believing they did enough."

He placed a hand on Hytar's shoulder.

"Lieutenant. Whatever happens in the coming weeks, remember this: the Umin count victories the same way. They calculate our casualties. They measure our will. They believe that, at some point, our mathematics will align with theirs—because they believe everyone is ultimately selfish, ultimately rational, ultimately *expendable*."

His eyes, in the dim light of the war room, seemed to glow faintly—the mark of a Preceptor who had looked too long into the fog of probability.

"Do not let them win that calculation. Do not let the numbers be the only story."

---

### Scene 9: The Promise

Outside the war room, in the corridor that led to the barracks, Nightingale and Drift found themselves walking together.

"You surprised them," Nightingale observed. "Offering to guide the deep insertion. Most would have stayed in the shadows."

Drift's expression was unreadable. "Shadows have a way of becoming prisons. And prisons have a way of making you forget what the sun looks like."

"You believe in this operation?"

Drift stopped walking. For a moment, they seemed to be looking not at Nightingale, but at something far beyond the walls of the Lymph Node.

"I believe," Drift said slowly, "that Phoenix was never theirs to keep. It was taken by surprise, by fog, by silence. It can be taken back by fire, by noise, by *questions*."

"And what question will you ask, when the time comes?"

Drift turned to face Nightingale fully. In their eyes was something that might have been hope, or might have been grief, or might have been both at once.

"I will ask the question I could not ask three years ago, when my colleagues were dragged away in the silence. I will ask: *'Did you believe you had no choice?'* And if the answer is yes—" Drift's voice caught, just slightly. "Then I will tell them that they were wrong. There is always a choice. The fog is not inevitable. The silence is not permanent. Someone can always choose to *speak*."

They began walking again, their footsteps echoing in the corridor that led to a war that had already begun.

## Epilogue: The Phoenix Directive

*Six weeks later, Operation Phoenix Liberation would begin.*

*Colonel Sable's Third Mobile Regiment would cut the Subclavian Line in forty-seven hours, establishing the Junction of Four Arteries as an Alopesian stronghold.*

*Nightingale's deep insertion teams would neutralize six of the seven Silencing Fog nodes—one team would be lost near the **Arterial Gallery**, and Nightingale herself would complete the seventh node solo, barely escaping with shrapnel in her shoulder.*

*Drift's guidance would prove invaluable, identifying three booby traps and two hidden patrol routes that would have cost the insertion teams dearly.*

*Phase Three—the Bone Marrow Gambit—would be the bloodiest of the operation. Colonel Sable would lose two hundred and twelve soldiers crossing the Calcified Flats. But the urban corridor would be reached, and the visible threat would force the Umin garrison to commit their reserves.*

*And in Phase Four—Operation Quietus—Lieutenant Hytar's unit would breach the Command Spire, neutralize Mute's informant network, and broadcast the question:*

***"What are you fighting for?"***

*Some Umin soldiers would laugh. Some would hesitate. And some—a handful, but enough—would lay down their arms and walk away.*

*Phoenix would fall.*

*But that is a story for another time.*

*For now, in the quiet before the storm, the defenders of the Lymph Node made their preparations.*

*The liberation was not yet achieved.*

*But it was being prepared.*
