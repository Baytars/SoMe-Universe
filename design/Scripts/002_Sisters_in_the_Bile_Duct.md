## PART I: THE DESCENT

The ultrasound probe swept through the bile duct like a lighthouse beam through fog.

"Contact. Bearing 030, range 400 microns. Multiple targets. Praziquantel damage confirmed."

Shim Beley's voice crackled through the neural link, cool and clinical. Her fingers danced across the haptic interface, translating sonar echoes into tactical data. The Drift hummed between them—a river of shared consciousness where her thoughts bled into her sister's.

Kim Berry felt the data flow, felt Beley's analytical precision crystallize around her like a second skin. But something else surged through the Drift from Berry's end. A current of protectiveness that bordered on suffocation.

"Unnie, let me handle the approach. You stay on analysis."

"I'm perfectly capable of approaching targets, Berry."

"I know, but—I'll go first. Just in case."

"In case of what?" Beley's voice was flat. Not angry. Just... tired. "In case the damaged parasites suddenly develop combat capabilities I can't handle?"

"In case something goes wrong."

Something always goes wrong, Berry. That's why we prepare. Beley's thought rippled through the Drift, tinged with something that might have been resignation. You keep trying to shield me from a universe I've already mapped.

The Jaeger—a Mark IV Combat Frame christened *Hwangchon* (황천, "Yellow Spring")—hung suspended in the bile flow. Its ultrasound array painted the darkness in shades of blue and gold. Around them, the hepatic architecture loomed: hexagonal lobules, central veins, the endless branching of the biliary tree.

And ahead, in the shadows of a dilated duct segment, the enemy waited.

*Clonorchis sinensis*. Liver flukes. Ten centimeters of hermaphroditic parasitism, flayed open by praziquantel bombardment. The drug had done its work—tegument disrupted, musculature paralyzed, organs exposed. But the worms still lived. And in their dying, they released antigens.

That was why the Shim sisters had been deployed. Heavy combat chassis with surgical attachments. Find the wounded. Finish them. Prevent systemic anaphylaxis from antigen flood.

"Targets confirmed." Beley's voice again. "Four adults. Severe tegumental damage. Minimal motility—"

"Unnie, behind us!"

Berry's instincts fired before the words left her mouth. A fifth fluke—unaccounted, undamaged, *alive*—surged from behind the carcass of its fellows. The thing was massive, flattened, hermaphroditic gonads pulsing with obscene vitality.

The Drift *screamed*.

---

## PART II: THE BREACH

"Unnie, get back! GET BACK!"

"Berry, we need to flank—"

"I said GET BACK!"

Kim Berry seized control of the Jaeger's leg actuators, overriding the neural handshake. The *Hwangchon* lurched backward, shielding Beley's position, its ultrasound probe swinging wildly. The movement was violent enough to rupture the fragile consensus of the Drift.

Beley felt it happen. The river of shared thought became a rapids. Her consciousness slammed against her sister's in a collision of panic and fury.

*You're overriding me!*

*I'm protecting you!*

*I don't NEED your protection! I never have!*

*You're too focused on analysis—you don't see threats until they're on top of you!*

*I see EVERYTHING, Berry! That's the POINT!*

The living fluke struck.

Not the Jaeger. The *corpses*.

Its oral sucker latched onto the nearest praziquantel-ravaged body and *tore*. Ruptured tegument split open. A flood of somatic antigens—proteins, glycoproteins, metabolic waste—erupted into the bile duct.

And then the *Hwangchon*'s ultrasound array caught the full blast of the antigen cloud.

The machine didn't fail. The pilots did.

The Drift collapsed.

---

## PART III: THE DARKNESS

When Beley's vision cleared, she was alone.

The *Hwangchon* listed in the bile flow, half its systems dark. The neural link—her connection to Berry—was static. Empty. A phone line cut at the source.

"Berry? Berry, respond. BERRY!"

Nothing.

The Jaeger's external cameras flickered. Through the static, she saw the damage. The antigen flood had triggered a local inflammatory cascade. Complement proteins. Cytokine storm. The bile duct was becoming a war zone, and she was caught in the crossfire.

And the fluke was still there.

The surviving adult had abandoned its dead brethren. It moved with terrible purpose, hermaphroditic body undulating through the chaos. Its oral sucker found purchase on the *Hwangchon*'s hull.

Then the other sucker—the ventral one—found *her*.

Not the Jaeger. The neural interface port. The connection point where the Drift lived.

*No.*

The fluke began to feed.

Beley's last coherent thought, before the parasite's neural invasion consumed her consciousness, was not fear for herself.

It was *I was right. I didn't need her protection.*

And then, quieter: *But I wanted it anyway.*

---

## PART IV: THE INTERVENTION

Lieutenant Roi Hytar had seen sibling rivalries destroy Drift pairs before.

She stood in Central Command, watching the biomarkers cascade across the tactical display. Two heart rates, asynchronous. Two neural signatures, decoupled. One rapidly losing consciousness.

"They're going to lose her," the medical officer said. "The fluke is tapping directly into her neural architecture. We need to abort."

"Abort means leaving Beley out there alone." Hytar's voice was flat. Clinical. Military.

"Belayed orders, Lieutenant. We pull them out now, or we lose them both."

Hytar turned to the communications array. Her fingers found the override codes.

"I'm not pulling them out. I'm putting them *back together*."

She opened a channel. Not to the Jaeger—to the Drift anchor. The failsafe point where pilot consciousness could be re-synchronized.

The channel connected.

---

## PART V: THE CONFRONTATION

"Staff Sergeant Berry. This is Lieutenant Hytar, Central Command."

Static. Then, faintly: "Lieutenant. Beley's—Beley's been captured. The fluke, it's—"

"I know. And I know you can hear me because your Drift signature is still anchored. Which means you're still in the system. Which means you can still feel her."

Silence.

"Berry, your sister is dying. The fluke is feeding on her neural patterns. You're the only one close enough to reach her."

"I tried—I tried to protect her—I always try—and she doesn't even—"

"She doesn't what?"

"She doesn't *need* me." Berry's voice cracked. "She never has. She's brilliant, she sees things I can't even imagine, she speaks in music and I'm just—I'm just the one who translates for everyone else. I thought if I could just *keep her safe*—"

"Staff Sergeant." Hytar's voice sharpened. "Listen to me. Your sister doesn't need your protection. She's told you that, what, a hundred times?"

"Yes."

"And you didn't believe her."

"I thought she was just—being modest. Or hiding how scared she was."

"Has it occurred to you that she's *not scared*? That she's not hiding anything? That when she says she doesn't need protection, she means it literally?"

Silence. Longer this time.

"She... she never asked for help. Not once. In all our years together."

"No. She didn't." Hytar watched the biofeeds. Berry's heart rate was stabilizing. "Your sister is a genius, Berry. Geniuses often don't need help—they need *partnership*. There's a difference. You've been treating her like someone to shield. She's been waiting for someone to trust."

"I... I don't know how to do that. I've always been the one who—"

"Then learn. Right now. In the next sixty seconds, or you lose her forever." Hytar's voice softened. Millimeters. "Find her consciousness in the Drift. Don't try to rescue her. Just... *join* her. Share the fear. Share the vulnerability. She's been alone in her own head for her entire life, Berry. Show her she doesn't have to be."

---

## PART VI: THE RENDEZVOUS

In the chaos of the inflammatory storm, Berry found her sister.

The Drift was a shattered mirror, but the pieces still reflected. She followed the fragments of Beley's consciousness—fragments of crystalline thought, fragments of musical patterns, fragments of profound loneliness.

*Unnie.*

*...Berry?*

*I'm here. I'm not leaving. I'm not... I'm not trying to protect you anymore.*

*You never had to.*

*I know. I'm sorry. I thought you were fragile. But you're not—you're the strongest person I know. You've been alone in your head this whole time, and I kept trying to put walls around you instead of just... being there.*

*...*

*Unnie?*

*You're late.* The thought was tinged with something that might have been amusement. *I've been waiting for you to join me for twenty-three years.*

Then take my hand.

The neural link surged. Two consciousnesses merged—not smoothly, not cleanly, but *completely*. The fear was shared. The loneliness was shared. The love was shared.

And in that unity, the *Hwangchon* reactivated.

The Jaeger's arm—the one Berry had fought to control—swung upward. But this time, she didn't act alone. She felt Beley's analytical precision guide her aim, felt the musical patterns that predicted the fluke's next move.

Together, they found the weak point.

*Now.*

They cut as one.

---

## PART VII: THE REUNION

Central Command debriefing room. Fourteen hours later.

The Shim sisters sat side by side on a medical cot, IV lines trailing from their arms. Antihistamines. Corticosteroids. The pharmacological aftermath of near-anaphylaxis.

Berry had her head on Beley's shoulder. Beley had her arms around her sister's waist. Neither spoke. Neither needed to.

Lieutenant Hytar stood in the doorway, tablet in hand.

"Medical clears you for light duty in 72 hours. Full duty pending psychological evaluation." She paused. "The evaluation is a formality. You passed the real test in that duct."

Berry looked up. "Lieutenant. We—you saved us. If you hadn't made me see—"

"I gave you a push. You did the rest." Hytar's expression softened again. That millimeter. "Your sister's been alone in her own head her whole life. You've been trying to protect someone who didn't need protecting—but who desperately needed *company*. There's a difference."

She turned to leave.

"Lieutenant?"

Hytar paused.

"Thank you. For making me see her. Really see her."

"You already did. You just needed permission to stop being her shield and start being her partner."

A ghost of a smile. "Rest. You've earned it."

## EPILOGUE

In the quiet of the recovery ward, Berry tightened her arms around her sister.

"I'm sorry," she whispered. "For not trusting you. For assuming you needed me to fight your battles."

"You were right about one thing." Beley's voice was quiet, amused. "I did need you. Just not the way you thought."

"What do you mean?"

Beley turned to look at her sister—really look, the way she analyzed genomic sequences, the way she heard musical patterns in chaos.

"I've been alone for twenty-three years, Berry. I speak in music, and most people hear noise. I see patterns in everything, and most people see chaos. I don't need protection from the world. I need someone who *understands* the world the way I do."

"I don't understand music the way you do, unnie."

"No. But you understand *me*. You translate my noise into words everyone else can hear. You fight the battles I'm too lost in thought to see coming." Beley smiled—a rare, genuine smile. "You're not my shield. You're my interpreter. My partner. My *sister*."

Berry felt tears sting her eyes. "I thought you were fragile."

"I thought I didn't need anyone. We were both wrong."

Outside the window, the artificial sky of the base cycled through a simulated sunset. Orange and gold, like bile ducts under ultrasound. Like the warmth of a sister's hand in yours.

"Unnie?"

"Hmm?"

"I love you. Not because you need me to protect you. Just because you're you."

Beley pulled her closer. Through the Drift—the real one, the one that lived in their hearts—Berry felt her sister's loneliness finally, fully ease.

"I love you too, dongsaeng. You're late. But you're here now."

"That's all that matters."

## NOTES ON MEDICAL ACCURACY

### Praziquantel Mechanism
Praziquantel causes rapid muscle contraction and tegumental damage in trematodes. The drug disrupts calcium homeostasis, leading to spastic paralysis and exposure of parasite antigens. This antigen release can trigger immune-mediated inflammation—a known clinical concern during treatment of heavy parasite burdens.

### *Clonorchis sinensis* Biology
- Hermaphroditic liver fluke, 10-25mm length
- Oral and ventral suckers for attachment
- Parasitizes bile ducts, causing cholangitis, cholangiocarcinoma risk
- Praziquantel is standard treatment; 75mg/kg single dose

### Inflammatory Response to Antigen Release
- Complement activation
- Cytokine cascade (IL-5, IL-13, IgE elevation in chronic infection)
- Potential for systemic anaphylaxis in heavy burdens
- Hepatic inflammation may exacerbate cholangitis

### Korean Cultural Elements
- *Unnie* (언니): Older sister, used by females addressing older females
- *Dongsaeng* (동생): Younger sibling
- *Hwangchon* (황천): "Yellow Spring," Korean Buddhist concept of afterlife/underworld
- Traditional medicine integration in Kim Berry's character background
