# LORE FILE: THE ALPSER SCHISM (阿普瑟分流)
## Classification: RESTRICTED // CRADLE INNER COURT × ALOPES COUNTER-INTELLIGENCE ARCHIVES

*Cross-filed history of the single mother-civilization that, in the Crimson Winter, split three ways — into the Duchy of Alopes, the Empire of Aukin, and the most hated stratum within Umin itself.*

---

## 1. DESIGNATION

**Mother-civilization**: the **Alpser** (阿普瑟) — the pre-Alopes peoples of the snow mountains of the lung, Continent Thoracica. "Alpser" is the ethnonym of the old civilization; **"Alopes"** is the name the southern refugees later gave their re-founded state. The Duchy is not the whole of the lineage. It is one branch — the branch that walked south.

**Capital of the old civilization**: **Phoenix**, in the high snow ranges above the great lung. (See [Phoenix](../Locations/Phoenix.md).)

**The defining catastrophe**: the **Crimson Winter (2295)** — Umin's Black Death, pneumonic plague made into biological warfare, fought primarily in and through the lung. (See [Duchy of Alopes](../SoMe_Design/Factions/Duchy_of_Alopes.md), *The Crimson Winter — Umin's Black Death*.)

**The Schism**: in the Winter the Alpser people did not fall as one. They split into **three streams**:

| Stream | Fate | Modern successor |
|---|---|---|
| **The Southbound** — refugees under Victoria | Migrated to Sacrum, held the line, re-founded the state | **Duchy of Alopes** |
| **The Awakened** — penitents who refused to leave | Held the old ground outside Phoenix, converted, built a theocracy | **Empire of Aukin** |
| **The Unrepentant** — the reactionary old order | Submitted to Umin, turned against their own | **"Alopes in Error"** — the Alpser strata inside Umin |

### The War for the Mother-Name

After the Winter the two branches that still claimed a legitimate inheritance — the Southbound state and the northern penitents — both asserted they were the true continuation of the mother-civilization. To distinguish themselves from the other, each was compelled both to coin a name for itself and to fasten a name on its rival:

| Faction | Self-name | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| The Southbound state | **Alopes** (takes the mother-name outright) | the re-founded, error-corrected civilization |
| The northern penitents | **Alpsor / Alpsox** (阿普索) — *Alopes Orthodox* | the faithful remnant that never abandoned the sacred ground |
| (the Umin-submitted branch) | styled by the Southbound as **"Alopes in Error"** | the strayed, erring lineage |

The northerners read the Southbound state's adoption of the bare mother-name as usurpation — a branch that had *walked away* presuming to call itself the whole. The Southbound in turn read "Orthodox" as the old hierarchy's refusal to confess the Hunt. The specific slur the Alpsor level back at the Southbound is left unrecorded in these archives \[name reserved\]; what is canon is the symmetry — *two heirs, one mother-name, each calling itself the orthodox and the other the deviation.* Note that "**Alpser**" remains the scholarly name for the pre-Winter civilization as a whole, not the self-designation of either post-Winter claimant.

## 2. THE OLD ALPSER CIVILIZATION

Long before the Winter, the Alpser peoples lived among the snow mountains of the lung, where the air came thin and clean off the heights. Phoenix was their ruling center — a high-plateau city of updrafts and clear sky, sacred to a bird that rose again from ash.

### The Winged Herb-Wives

Alpser society drew its medicine and its chief cultural image from its women. The herb-wives (the later "winged women," 飞翼女医) went up onto the snow slopes on **flying wings**, gathering alpine herbs and boiling them down into medicines in baskets slung beside them as they flew. This is the historical truth beneath the slander recorded elsewhere as **"witches riding broomsticks"**: there was no broomstick and no sorcery — there were healers trailing a **basket of herbs** through the mountain air. (The image is a distant, distorted memory of the winged basket; the real-world echo is the folk memory of women herbalists, of the Ming-dynasty female physicians and the European village cunning-women alike.)

This tradition is the seed of a future **herbalist faction (中草药阵营)** within the setting — an indigenous pharmacopoeia current that runs alongside the radiological priesthood; reserved for later development.

### The Sweepers of the Outer Snow

Outside the city, in the windward passages of the lung, lived a second order: the **Sweepers (清道夫)** — mountain ascetics whose cellular archetype is the **ciliated epithelial cell**. Their work was to sweep the "snow of the sky-fallen stones" — the dust and ash that drifted down onto the lung — back up and out of the body, on the moving stair of hair-like cilia that never rests. They were a low, half-monastic order: cleaners, not rulers. Phoenix governed; the Sweepers swept.

Among the Sweepers a minority grew fascinated not with the dust itself but with the **mineral content inside the sky-fallen stones**. These became the first **alchemists** — and the ancestors, by a long road, of the radiological priesthood (see §4).

### The Two Faces of the Lung — Snow and Water

The lung is not snow only. The high passes are the windward snows; the lower lung is a vast living hydrology, and the Alpser peoples named its vessels as a geography of water:

- **Streams (溪)** — the capillary beds
- **Lakes (湖)** — the alveoli, the still airsacs where breath meets blood
- **Rivers (河)** — the small arteries and veins
- **Seas (海)** — the great vessels

So the mountains had their **fishermen (渔民)** as well as their sweepers and their flying healers: lake-folk who worked the alveolar shallows. (The parents of **Yuan Yosemite** were alveolar-cell fishermen of one such northern lake-village; see her [personnel file](../Characters/Yuan_Yosemite.md).) The snow above and the water below are the same organ seen from two altitudes.

The body's own clearance is the converse of an avalanche. Mucociliary clearance carries dust and the trapped dead **upward, against gravity**, out of the lung — a *reverse avalanche* of "snow." Any pathogen that attempts an **air-drop into the lung** and is caught in the ascending mucus-sheet is swept back up into that storm and buried in it; invader and expectorated snow leave the body together. The Alpser name for an airborne assault swallowed by the escalator was simply *"lost in the snow."*

## 3. THE CRIMSON WINTER AND THE HUNT

In 2295 Umin loosed the Black Death as a weapon. Because it struck first and worst as **pneumonic plague**, the lung — the Alpser homeland — became the primary battlefield. The "Crimson Winter" is the Alopesian name for the season everything died.

But the plague was only the first blade. The second was the **Hunt (猎巫运动, the Witch Hunt)**: Umin turned the civilization against its own healers. The winged herb-wives — the women who held the society's medical knowledge — were accused of causing the very plague they were fighting. (This is the in-world transposition of the historical European witch-hunts, which followed the Black Death and preceded the Renaissance; here the "Renaissance" is the later medical re-founding in Sacrum.)

The mechanism of the collapse was therefore twofold:

1. **The destruction of medicine.** With the healers hunted, the pharmacopoeia died with its keepers. When the second wave came there was no one left who knew the herbs.
2. **Genetic susceptibility, unmedicated.** The epidemic disproportionately killed males (the constitutional susceptibility recorded in Alopesian demographic tables); stripped of the medicine that had always compensated, the male population was — in the cold phrase of the Cradle archivists — *"easily harvested by Umin for want of a healer."*

By the depth of the Winter the old order had fractured along the lines drawn in §1.

## 4. THE THREE STREAMS

### 4.1 The Southbound — The Duchy of Alopes

Most survivors fled south toward Sacrum. **Dutchess Victoria** did not flee. Like the Ming general Yu Qian at the siege of the capital, she held the old capital Phoenix with the remnant — calling on the men who had at last *awakened* to stand beside the women who would not surrender an inch of the homeland: *the幡然醒悟 (the awakened men) and the寸土必争 (the women who contest every inch of ground)*.

That defense is the political foundation of everything that followed:

- It is the basis on which Victoria later **continued to employ men such as Yuan Foo (袁甫)** in the highest offices. Men had not been the enemy; the *unawakened* old order had been. Loyalty and competence, not sex, became the test — the root of Alopes's meritocratic matriarchy.
- When the immediate crisis passed, Victoria understood that the mass of survivors had now put down roots among the **Sacrum clans**, and that the old Phoenix elite could not rule a migrated people from a ruined northern city without breeding resentment. To reconcile the grieving, resistant Sacrum portion of the population, she **moved the seat of government south to Anshington**, the administrative center of the Sacrum lands, and continued to rule from there.
- Phoenix was retained as a **spiritual totem** — but, cut off from the new population center and guarded in the north by the Awakened, it became in practical terms an **enclave (飞地)**. In later decades the city deliberately built up **modern aviation**, in part to shed the old shadow of "witches on broomsticks": to prove that the flying heirs of the herb-wives now flew **machines of science and rescue**, not baskets of folk magic. (See [Phoenix](../Locations/Phoenix.md).)

The re-founded state named itself **Alopes** and defined itself against the old civilization's failure. The official phrase is **"Alopes in Error"** — a term with two deliberate edges:

- **Outward**, it is the Duchy's doctrinal label for the **Unrepentant reactionary branch** that served Umin: the strayed, erring Alopes.
- **Inward**, it is a self-warning: the whole old civilization once erred in the Hunt, and the new state claims no innocence, only the discipline not to repeat the error.

### 4.2 The Awakened — The Empire of Aukin

Not everyone walked south. A remnant of penitents — Sweepers, disillusioned alchemists, and those who had taken part in or merely witnessed the Hunt and could not forgive themselves — **stayed on the old ground outside Phoenix**, guarding the approaches to the fallen capital. They call themselves the **Alpsor / Alpsox (阿普索)**, *Alopes Orthodox* — the faithful who never left — and contest the Southbound state's sole claim to the mother-name (see §1, *The War for the Mother-Name*). They are the reason an enclave survived at all: Aukin was founded, in its earliest military form, to **stand guard over Phoenix**.

They entered the ancient mountain monasteries of the lung and converted, naming the old civilization's crime an **Original Sin (原罪)**: not the plague, which was Umin's, but the Hunt — a people murdering its own healers. From this penitence grew the **Dhyana doctrine (谛)**: *know the self; the foreign reveals itself by contrast; the worst wars are lost at birth, when defenders are made without ever being taught who they are.* (See [Empire of Aukin](./Aukin_Empire.md), *The Cradle*.)

The spiritual-economic genealogy of these penitents runs in one unbroken, deliberately ironic line:

1. **Sweepers (纤毛细胞)** — ciliated ascetics cleaning the sky-fallen dust from the lung.
2. **Alchemists** — the minority who studied the mineral content of that dust.
3. **Worshippers of the Heavy Metal Pantheon** — in the stones they found the ancient metal gods (the Atomic Ancients) and built cathedrals to them; theology grew out of mining.
4. **Disenchantment** — the old gods were demystified into physics: isotopes, decay, the invisible fire.
5. **Radiological "theology"** — the Order of the Atom and its sects (Khonsu, the Ashborn, the Chained Flame, the Silent Watch), a priesthood that wields a disenchanted atom as though it were still divine. *(See [The Curie Legacy](../Characters/The_Curie_Legacy.md), "The Immortal Alchemist"; [Radiologist Class](../Characters/Radiologist_Class.md).)*

> The Zen theocracy and the nuclear temple orders are therefore not two imported religions bolted together. They are **two growths from the same penitent root**: the meditative doctrine of the self, and the worshippers of the atom, reconciled in Thoracica through the *suijaku* (本地垂迹) arrangement.

#### Veneration of the Healers — the White-Clad Angels

In Aukin doctrine the murdered herb-wives are not a shame to be buried but saints to be recovered. The penitents gave the winged women a name of their own: the **White-Clad Angels (白衣天使)** — white-robed, sky-going mercies who once carried medicine through the mountain air and were burned for it. To name the healers angels is the reverse face of the Original Sin: a people that once called its healers witches now calls them angels, and builds the cult around the apology. The white healing habit descends from this veneration (the Khonsu knight Sister Franklin wears its modern form). The phrase is deliberately beautiful — Aukin's theologians hold that a crime against mercy can only be atoned in a language more beautiful than the crime.

### 4.3 The Unrepentant — Alpser-in-Error Within Umin

The third stream did not migrate and did not repent. The reactionary wing of the old Alpser order — the male-dominated hierarchy that had led or profited from the Hunt — **chose submission to Umin**. They are the "Alopes in Error" in its outward, accusatory sense, though they call themselves the true Alpser and the southern Duchy the renegades.

Their women live under a doubled oppression: coerced by Umin, they have **internalized the very stigmatization the Hunt first imposed on the herb-wives**, and now submit absolutely to male command. The flying basket of the healer has its darkest descendant here: the hereditary **Typhi carriers** — women who carry plague without suffering it, hidden, patient, indistinguishable from the healthy. (Historical archetype: Mary Mallon. See [Umin Typhi Subfaction](../Factions/Umin_Typhi_Subfaction.md).) The basket that once carried herbs now carries contagion.

Under Umin's direction this branch is tasked with a very specific strategic wound: **the penetration of Aukin and the breaking of its doctrinal heart (破道心)**. They understand, as only kin can, that Aukin's power is not military but educational and spiritual — that to corrupt the Cradle is to make an entire generation unable to tell self from foreign.

**Operation DOPPELGÄNGER was conceived against this background.** The Typhi operative **MARY-07**, wearing the face of a Cradle alumna (Chief Medical Advisor Yuan Yukio), ran a 68-day infiltration whose ultimate target was not an Alopesian ministry but the **Chair of Foreign Selves in Thoracica** — the destruction of Vasud Stuart, the teacher whose whole office was *recognizing the fake*. It was a crime with a half-century of family history behind it: the heirs of the women the old Alpser order once burned, sent to murder the conscience of the penitents who once did the burning. (See [Vasud Stuart](../SoMe百科/Vasud_Stuart.md); [The Generalizer](../Characters/The_Generalizer.md).)

## 6. DOCTRINAL SUMMARY

- One mother-civilization (**Alpser**), three answers to the same guilt: the Southbound **rebuilt**, the Awakened **repented and guarded**, the Unrepentant **submitted and infiltrated**.
- The Duchy of Alopes is a medical matriarchy because its medicine was murdered and had to be rebuilt, chiefly by the survivors the Hunt had failed to kill.
- The Empire of Aukin is a penitent's country: its ciliated Sweepers and its radiological priests descend from the same disenchanted, guilt-bearing root, and its entire educational project is an attempt to ensure no future army ever fails to recognize the self.
- The deepest feud of the setting is not Alopes–Umin or Anstar–Umin. It is the feud **inside one family**: the penitent brother and the unrepentant brother, the healer's basket and the carrier's basket, the Cradle and the doppelgänger.
