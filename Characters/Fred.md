# Fred — Forest Ranger

**Role:** Forest Ranger  
**Age:** 52  
**Status:** Active  
**Location:** Forest sector near Sacrum-Cervical border  
**Affiliation:** Neutral (Civilian) / Former Phoenix Resident

---

## Background

Fred served as a maintenance worker in Phoenix General Hospital before the city's fall. His son, Daniel, was born with **Marfan syndrome** — a genetic disorder affecting connective tissue, characterized by tall stature, elongated limbs, arachnodactyly (spider-like fingers), and most critically, cardiovascular vulnerabilities including aortic aneurysms.

In 2341, when Umin forces launched their assault on Phoenix (later known as the **Phoenix Incident**), Daniel was undergoing emergency aortic aneurysm repair surgery at Phoenix General. The operation was interrupted when *Pneumoniae Panzergruppe* armor units breached the hospital perimeter.

In the chaos, Fred was separated from his son. Evacuation protocols forced him onto the last transport out of the city. Daniel was left behind — last seen on the operating table, unconscious and intubated.

---

## Current Status

Fred now works as a forest ranger in a remote sector near the Sacrum-Cervical continental border. The isolation suits him — he avoids reminders of Phoenix, yet obsessively monitors radio transmissions for any news of liberation efforts or missing persons lists.

He has refused multiple reassignment offers from Alopes authorities, choosing instead to remain in his forest outpost where he can think, wait, and hope.

---

## The Slender Man Sighting

In late 2345, during a routine patrol, Fred observed a figure in a forest clearing approximately 200 meters from his position.

**His report (never officially filed):**

> "Tall. Too tall. Limbs... wrong proportions. No face that I could see. But the way it moved — the way the arms hung —  
> *[REDACTED — Ranger's personal notes]*  
> It looked like... like Daniel. The way he stood when he was tired. The way his shoulders curved forward."

Fred did not pursue. He did not report. He returned to his station and spent three days reviewing old photographs of his son.

---

## The Truth (GM/Player Hidden Information)

Unknown to Fred, Daniel survived the Phoenix Incident — but not as a free person.

When *Pneumoniae Panzergruppe* soldiers entered the operating theater, they found a half-conscious patient with grotesquely elongated limbs, fingers like spider legs, and a chest scar from the interrupted surgery. Initially, they raised their weapons to deliver a "mercy kill."

**Then the transmission came.**

Umin Fear Technology Division had been monitoring hospital feeds for subjects with extreme physical anomalies. Marfan syndrome — with its distinctive body proportions — was a perfect template for their Fear Construct program. The soldiers received new orders: *"Secure the specimen for Project THIN MAN. Highest priority."*

Daniel was evacuated to a Umin bioengineering facility where he underwent:
- Surgical limb elongation (beyond natural Marfan proportions)
- Facial feature removal (surgical neutralization of facial muscles and structures)
- Neural reprogramming (fear projection implantation)
- Hormonal modification (growth hormone acceleration)

The result: a living Fear Construct — **Slender Man** — derived from a real human being with a real medical condition.

---

## Operation Trinity

Umin Fear Technology Division deliberately engineered Fred's encounter with his transformed son. The operation was designated **Trinity** — referencing the three entities now intertwined:

1. **The Father** — Fred, the target
2. **The Son** — Daniel, the original human template
3. **The Construct** — Slender Man, the weaponized urban legend

The Trinity concept serves multiple purposes:
- **Field Test:** Verify the psychological impact of Slender Man on individuals with personal emotional connections to the original human template
- **Propaganda Value:** Document Fred's psychological breakdown for internal reports to the "上峰" (High Command)
- **Strategic Naming:** "Trinity" echoes the first nuclear weapons test — signaling that this represents a new category of psychological mass destruction

---

## Personality & Psychology

- **Obsessive Hope:** Fred has not accepted Daniel's death. He maintains a carefully organized archive of every Phoenix-related news broadcast, missing persons list, and AAA liberation progress report.

- **Avoidant Attachment:** He refuses to form new close relationships, fearing that hope for Daniel's return will make him vulnerable to disappointment.

- **Rationalization:** When he saw Slender Man, his mind immediately sought a rational explanation — perhaps it was a trick of light, perhaps it was a hallucination from sleep deprivation. He has not consciously acknowledged the possibility that it was Daniel.

- **Guilt:** Fred carries survivor's guilt. He questions whether he should have stayed in the hospital, whether he could have saved Daniel, whether his escape was cowardice.

## Potential Story Hooks

1. **AAA Investigation:** A recovery team searching for missing persons in liberated Phoenix finds hospital records indicating Daniel was taken alive by Umin forces. They attempt to locate Fred.

2. **Slender Man Confrontation:** Fred encounters Slender Man again — this time in a situation where he must choose between attacking the construct or trying to communicate with what remains of his son.

3. **Umin Psychological Warfare:** Umin releases doctored footage of Slender Man specifically targeting Fred, using the father-son connection to terrorize Alopes border communities.

4. **Medical Discovery:** An AAA medical team analyzing Slender Man's anatomical structure recognizes the Marfan syndrome skeletal markers and cross-references with missing persons databases.

## Notes for Writers

- Fred's story bridges the gap between **horror** and **tragedy**. Slender Man is not just a monster — it is a crime against a human being.

- The Marfan syndrome connection grounds the supernatural horror in real medical pathology, making the fear more visceral for players/ readers with medical knowledge.

- Fred's recognition of his son's posture in Slender Man's silhouette is the emotional pivot point — it transforms a "monster sighting" into a "recognition scene" with devastating implications.

- The "上峰" designation suggests Umin's internal power structure mimics real-world authoritarian bureaucracies, adding political satire to the horror.
