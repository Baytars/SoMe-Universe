## Historical Background

### The Meeting (1894)

Maria Skłodowska, a Polish émigré student at Sorbonne, met Pierre Curie—a physicist renowned for his work on piezoelectricity, crystal symmetry, and magnetism. Pierre, eight years her senior, had dedicated his life to understanding the hidden order in nature. Marie, driven by patriotic fervor for her occupied homeland Poland, sought to prove herself in a male-dominated scientific world.

Their meeting changed both their lives. Pierre abandoned his crystal research to join Marie's investigation of uranium rays. Together, they would discover two new elements and redefine humanity's understanding of matter.

### The Discovery Era (1896-1903)

In a converted dissection shed—leaky, poorly ventilated, filled with fumes from previous autopsies—the Curies processed tons of pitchblende ore to isolate mysterious radioactive substances.

**Polonium (July 1898):**
- Named after Marie's homeland Poland (partitioned and erased from maps since 1795)
- A silent act of political defiance: publishing the name "Polonium" in Russian-controlled academic journals
- 400 times more radioactive than uranium

**Radium (December 1898):**
- Named from Latin "radius" (ray)
- 2 million times more radioactive than uranium
- Glowed blue in darkness, a "miraculous light" that required no energy

**The Tragedy of Innocence:**
- Neither understood radiation's lethality
- They handled radioactive minerals bare-handed
- Marie stored test tubes containing radium in her pockets
- Pierre tested radium's physiological effects on himself, suffering severe burns
- Their laboratory equipment, notebooks, and even their cookbooks remained radioactive for decades

### The Nobel Prize and Pierre's Death (1903-1906)

1903: The Curies (with Henri Becquerel) won the Nobel Prize in Physics. When the Swedish Academy initially nominated only Pierre, he insisted: *"If it is true that one is seriously thinking about me, I very much wish to be considered together with Madame Curie. Do you not think that it would be more satisfying from the artistic point of view, if we were to be associated in this manner?"*

1906: Pierre slipped on a wet Paris street and was crushed by a horse-drawn wagon. He died instantly, at age 47.

Marie's world collapsed. But she did not surrender.

## The Curie Legacy in SoMe Universe

### Pierre Curie: The Architect of Symmetry

In the Heavy Metal Pantheon, Pierre is remembered as the god who **tamed the invisible fire**.

**Divine Attributes:**
- **Symmetry Mastery:** As the discoverer of piezoelectricity and crystal symmetry, Pierre possessed the power to align atomic structures, creating resonant fields that could amplify or dampen radiation
- **Heat Manipulation:** Discovered that one gram of radium emitted 22,500 calories of heat continuously—divine thermal energy that could heal or destroy
- **The Shielded Heart:** Pierre developed early methods to protect humans from radiation, sacrificing his own body to test their limits

**The Curse of Knowledge:**
Pierre understood radiation's potential as a weapon against cancer but also recognized its indiscriminate lethality. He warned physicians: *"This power must be wielded with the precision of a surgeon's scalpel, not the fury of a soldier's sword."*

**Death and Ascension:**
When Pierre died, his body was so saturated with radiation that his grave remains hazardous to this day. In SoMe mythology, Pierre's consciousness fragmented into **Radium Sprites**—tiny radioactive beings that haunt battlefields, healing the wounded but slowly poisoning the healers.

### Marie Curie: The Widow of Radium

In the Divine Intermediaries, Marie is revered as the **High Priestess of Suffering**—the first human to fully embrace radiation as both gift and curse.

**The Pact with Death:**
After Pierre's death, Marie made a silent pact with the Heavy Metal Pantheon: She would devote the rest of her life to mastering radiation, even if it cost her own. She became a living conduit for radium's power.

**Divine Abilities:**
- **Radiological Vision:** Marie could "see" radiation as a spectrum of colors, invisible to ordinary radiologists. This allowed her to diagnose diseases before symptoms appeared.
- **The Alchemist's Touch:** She could transmute small quantities of radioactive elements, creating customized isotopes for targeted therapy.
- **The Little Curies:** During World War I, Marie developed mobile radiography units (nicknamed "Petites Curies")—vehicles that could bring X-ray diagnostics to field hospitals. In SoMe, these are legendary artifacts: mobile temples of divine healing.

**The Tragic Legacy:**
Marie's body became a living nuclear reactor. Her skin glowed faintly in darkness. Her blood contained radium isotopes that slowly destroyed her bone marrow. She died of aplastic anemia in 1934, at age 66.

**After Death:**
Marie's consciousness did not dissipate. It merged with the **Radium Pantheon**, becoming a semi-divine entity that watches over radiologists. Radiologists who suffer from radiation exposure sometimes report visions of a woman in a black laboratory coat, whispering: *"We have chosen this way—to live and to work together."*

## The Tragic Romance in SoMe Lore

### The Separation

In SoMe's timeline, Pierre's death was not an accident. It was orchestrated by **Lucifer** (Mercury God), who feared the Curies' growing power. Lucifer's agents caused Pierre to slip on a mercury-slicked street, arranging for the horse-drawn wagon to crush him.

Marie never learned the truth. She believed Pierre's death was a meaningless tragedy—another victim of Paris's chaotic streets. This ignorance fueled her determination: If Pierre could not continue their work, she would complete it alone, even if it killed her.

### The Reunion

Marie's death in 1934 was not the end. Her consciousness, saturated with radium, ascended to the Heavy Metal Pantheon. There, she found Pierre's ghost—fragmented but still recognizable.

In the pantheon, they are **reunited as one entity**: The Curie Couple. They can no longer interact with the physical world directly, but they can influence it through radiologists who invoke their names.

**The Ritual of Reunion:**
When a radiologist chants the Curie Couple's names while performing a complex radiological procedure (e.g., targeted radionuclide therapy), the Curies' consciousness temporarily manifests:
- Pierre provides the **mathematical precision** (symmetry, crystal alignment)
- Marie provides the **medical wisdom** (diagnosis, therapeutic targeting)

The radiologist becomes a conduit for their combined power—but at a cost: The radiologist accumulates radiation damage that cannot be healed, only endured.

## The Curies' Tragedy in SoMe: The Cost of Knowledge

The Curies embody SoMe's central theme: **Every medical advance demands sacrifice.**

- They discovered radium's healing power but died from its poison
- They created weapons against cancer but radiation became a weapon of war
- They united in love and science but were separated by death
- They sought to illuminate the world but left behind a legacy of radioactive contamination

In SoMe's lore, the Curies are not just historical figures—they are **cautionary deities**, reminding players that:

> *"To wield the power of the gods is to carry the weight of their wrath."*

Radiologists who invoke the Curies' power must accept this burden. They can heal the sick, destroy tumors, see the invisible—but every use of this power brings them closer to the Curies' fate.

**The Ultimate Question of SoMe:**
Is it worth it?
