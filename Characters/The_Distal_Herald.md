## CONCEPTUAL BASIS

The Distal Herald is the personification of a finding reported in *Science Immunology*: Kim Y, Kang I, … Lee HK, "The efficacy of immunotherapy in glioma requires distal B cell responses in tumor-draining lymph nodes," *Science Immunology* 11(121), 10 Jul 2026, DOI 10.1126/sciimmunol.adz2494 (KAIST). Anti–CTLA-4 checkpoint therapy in murine glioblastoma works only when B cells are activated in the tumor-draining **deep cervical lymph nodes**. Anti–CTLA-4 expands T follicular helper cells there, driving germinal-center B responses, IgG class switching, and glioma-reactive antibodies. Distally secreted IgG travels back into the tumor, tags glioma cells, and licenses intratumoral macrophages to phagocytose them. Mice lacking antibody-secreting cells gain no benefit from the blockade.

Anatomical route: ISF → CSF → meningeal lymphatic vessels (MLVs, dura mater) → deep cervical lymph nodes — the reason an organ once called "immune privileged" can still be answered by a writ drafted far from the battlefield.

Her doctrine in one sentence:

> *"The brain sends no army out. It sends a message. I read it at the border, copy it a thousand times, and send back a seal the devourers recognize."*

## NARRATIVE NOTES

She never enters the cranial vault herself. Her authority exists entirely at the distal relay; the effect of her work arrives back at the tumor as circulating IgG. This makes her a teaching figure for the humoral–cellular interplay: Tfh helps, B cell seals, macrophage executes — three roles, one writ.
