# THE DNA-KNITTING MOUSE

**Patron Saint of Experimental Sacrifice | Keeper of the Knitting Needles**

> *"She combines both the image of a laboratory mouse and a scientist, because they are interconnected and serve the same cause."* — Andrey Kharkevich, designer

---

## Overview

In the cosmic battlefield of SoMe, where medicine is warfare and the body is a universe, there exists a sacred figure that bridges the gap between the **known and the unknown**, the **scientist and the subject**, the **living and the sacrificed**: the **DNA-Knitting Mouse**.

She is not a legend—she is a **memorial**. Cast in bronze in the city of Novosibirsk, Russia, in 2013, she stands before the Institute of Cytology and Genetics, forever frozen in the act of **knitting a double helix of DNA**.

She is the **Patron Saint of all laboratory animals**—the mice and rats, the flies and worms, the dogs and monkeys who gave their bodies so that humanity might understand disease.

---

## Historical Origin: The Real Monument

### The Shrine in Novosibirsk

**Location:** Akademgorodok, Novosibirsk, Russia (in front of the Institute of Cytology and Genetics)

**The Statue:**
- A bronze laboratory mouse wearing **pince-nez glasses** perched on its nose
- The mouse holds **knitting needles** in its paws
- Between the needles, a double helix of **DNA** spirals upward
- The mouse sits on a **granite pedestal** 2.5 meters tall (the bronze mouse itself is 70 cm)

**The Symbolic Details:**

1. **The Glasses:** The mouse wears glasses, making her look like a **scientist**. The image blends the subject with the researcher—the mouse is not just a victim, but a **collaborator in discovery**.

2. **The Knitting Needles:** Knitting is a metaphor for **scientific labor**—threading understanding together one stitch at a time. The needles connect past discoveries to future breakthroughs.

3. **The DNA Helix:** The helix is **Z-DNA**—left-handed DNA, not the common B-DNA (right-handed). Z-DNA is **less understood** than B-DNA, symbolizing that there is still so much more to discover.

4. **The Granite Pedestal:** Solid, eternal, meant to last forever. The memorial is permanent.

**The Sacred Inscription:**
```
Monument of a laboratory mouse
The mouse holds needles in her hands, knitting the twin spiral of DNA
Exhibit of the Museum of the History of Genetics in Siberia
Author and artist A. Kharkevich
Sculptor A. Agrikolyansky
Foundryman M. Petrov
Installed by the Institute of Cytology and Genetics, Siberian Branch of the Russian Academy of Sciences
With the financial support of CJSC "Medico-Biological Union"
July 4, 2013
```

## In SoMe Universe

### Role: Patron Saint of the Lab Martyrs

In the SoMe universe, the DNA-Knitting Mouse is not just a memorial—she is a **living saint**, a spiritual embodiment of all laboratory animals.

**Celestial Domain:** The **Knitting Nebula**—a region of the cosmos where the threads of life are woven together

**Sacred Instruments:**
- **The Knitting Needles:** Forged from the same bronze as her statue, they can stitch wounds, repair DNA, and bind wounds
- **The Spectacles:** Allow her to see the genetic code—B-DNA, Z-DNA, and forms yet undiscovered
- **The Pedestal:** A mobile platform that grants her immunity to harm (she is already immortalized in bronze)

## Character Profile

### Name and Title

**Official Name:** The DNA-Knitting Mouse
**Also Known As:** The Knitting Mouse, Saint of the Needles, Patron of Sacrifice
**Designation:** LM-001 (Laboratory Martyr, First)

### Physical Appearance

- **Form:** A small laboratory mouse, approximately 15 cm long
- **Distinctive Features:** 
  - Large pince-nez glasses perched on the nose
  - Tiny paws holding silver knitting needles
  - A DNA helix trailing from the needles like an endless scarf
- **Coloration:** White fur (albino laboratory mouse), golden eyes behind the glasses
- **Attire:** A miniature lab coat, buttoned and crisp

### Personality

**Demeanor:** Quiet, contemplative, perpetually in the act of creation

**Core Traits:**
- **Patience:** She has been knitting since 2013 and will knit until the end of time
- **Dedication:** She serves a purpose she may not understand, but serves it completely
- **Sorrow:** Beneath her calm exterior lies the weight of countless sacrifices
- **Hope:** Every stitch of DNA is a promise that deaths were not in vain

**The Knitter's Paradox:**
> *"I knit the code of life, but I cannot change my own. I mend the wounds of others, but I am already frozen in bronze. This is my service: to remember, to connect, to promise that knowledge comes from sacrifice."*

## Abilities and Powers

### The Knitting Arts

**DNA Knitting:**
The Mouse can manipulate genetic material by "knitting" DNA strands together. She can:
- Repair broken genes
- Stitch together cellular wounds
- Create new genetic patterns from existing threads

**The Infinite Scarf:**
Her DNA helix is endless—stretching behind her like a scarf. This scarf:
- Can be used to bind enemies
- Carries the memory of every genetic discovery
- Glows with a soft bioluminescent light

### The Spectacles of Insight

**Vision Beyond Vision:**
Her pince-nez glasses allow her to see:
- The difference between B-DNA and Z-DNA
- Genetic mutations as "dropped stitches"
- The connections between genes as "pattern sequences"

### The Martyr's Blessing

**Immunity to Harm:**
Because she is already commemorated in bronze, the Mouse cannot be killed—only damaged. She regenerates instantly, returning to her immortal knitting.

**Sacrifice's Grace:**
When other laboratory animals face their end, the Mouse can appear to grant them:
- **Painless passing** (a final gift)
- **Memory preservation** (their contributions recorded)
- **Purpose revelation** (understanding their role in the greater design)

## Relationships

### With the Celestial Corps

**Respectful Distance:**
The Anatomists (now the Celestial Corps) owe their discoveries to the sacrifices of animals. The Mouse represents the debt owed to those who made mapping the body possible.

- **Leonardo da Vinci** studied the bodies of the dead; the Mouse's kin made such studies ethical
- **Vesalius** corrected Galen's errors; he did so using animal models before human dissection
- **William Harvey** proved circulation; he experimented on dogs and deer

**The Shared Truth:**
Both anatomists and martyrs serve the same cause: **understanding the body to save lives**. The Mouse acknowledges this, even if anatomists often forget.

### With HeLa

**Sisterhood of Sacrifice:**
HeLa and the Mouse share a unique bond—both are immortal, both serve science, both were used without full consent.

- HeLa is **alive** in death (her cells divide forever)
- The Mouse is **frozen** in life (she cannot age or die)

Together, they represent the spectrum of experimental sacrifice: **the cell that lives on** and **the subject immortalized in bronze**.

### With Laika

**The Pioneer and the Martyr:**
Laika was the first to go into space; the Mouse's kin were among the first to go into laboratories. Both represent sacrifice for human knowledge.

- Laika died in the **cold of space**
- The Mouse's kin died in the **cold of experiment**

They are **sisters in service**, honored together at the Shrine of Sacrifice.

## External Reference: The Artist Speaks

**Designer Andrey Kharkevich** on his creation:

*"It combines both the image of a laboratory mouse and a scientist, because they are interconnected and serve the same cause. The mouse is captured at the moment of scientific discovery. If you look closely at her eyes, you can see that this mouse has already come up with something. But the whole symphony of scientific discovery, joy, 'eureka!' has not yet sounded."*

**Sculptor Alexey Agrikolyansky** on his challenge:

*"It was not easy to do, because the mouse is still not a human being, and for her it was necessary to invent emotions, character, find something between a cartoon character and a real mouse, comply with its anatomical options."*
