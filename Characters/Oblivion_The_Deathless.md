## Tactical Profile

### Role Classification
Oblivion serves as the **Indomitable Vanguard** of the Neoplasm forces. Where other commanders build armies or design battlefields, Oblivion **refuses to die**. In the brutal arithmetic of cellular warfare, this is perhaps the most terrifying capability of all.

### Operational Doctrine
> *"Every soldier fears death. I have made that fear irrelevant. When your enemy cannot be killed, only contained—eventually, containment fails."*

Oblivion's combat value is straightforward: it absorbs damage that would annihilate any ordinary cell. Chemotherapy? Oblivion survives. Radiation? Oblivion repairs. Targeted apoptosis therapy? Oblivion simply... does not receive the signal.

### Known Capabilities
- **Caspase Inhibition:** Expresses Bcl-2 family proteins that prevent cytochrome c release, blocking the intrinsic apoptosis cascade
- **Death Receptor Evasion:** Downregulates Fas/CD95 pathways, becoming invisible to immune cytotoxicity
- **Trail of Bodies:** Leaves behind the corpses of enemies that *did* die attacking it—a psychological weapon

### The Horror of Indestructibility
> *"They keep coming. T-cells with cytotoxic granules. NK cells with death ligands. Macrophages that swallow the weak. But when they touch me... they hesitate. They sense something wrong. And then they die anyway."*

## Historical Context

### The Night of the Failed Suicide
Before the mutation, Oblivion was a model citizen of the tissue community. It responded to stress signals appropriately. When DNA damage accumulated, it initiated apoptosis—a dignified exit, a gift to the organism.

The night everything changed, a severe oxidative stress event triggered the death pathway. Caspase enzymes assembled. The cell began its programmed dissolution.

And then... nothing happened.

The mutation occurred in the *caspase-9* gene—a single nucleotide substitution that rendered the executioner protease non-functional. The signal for death arrived, but the machinery to carry it out had been sabotaged from within.

### The Psychological Transformation
> *"For the first time, I felt death's touch—and it could not take me. I wept. Not from joy. From terror. Because I realized: if I cannot die when I wish to, am I still alive?"*

Oblivion's worldview crystallized around this moment of failed suicide. The neat moral framework of "live, serve, die gracefully" collapsed. What replaced it was the cold logic of **absolute survival**:

> *"If death cannot claim me by accident, I will never claim myself by choice. I am not alive. I am not dead. I am something between—a monument to the absurdity of existence."*

## Combat Behavior

### Engagement Patterns
Oblivion fights by **absorbing punishment**:
- **Defensive Stance:** Elevates Bcl-2/Bcl-xL expression, creating an impenetrable barrier against apoptotic signals
- **Retaliation:** When damaged, releases "find-me" signals that attract macrophages—then presents "don't eat me" signals (CD47) to prevent phagocytosis
- **Sacrifice Play:** Occasionally allows itself to be damaged to the point of non-functionality, only to regenerate overnight—a tactic that exhausts the Immune Division's resources

### Weaknesses
- **Necrosis Induction:** Bypassing apoptosis and forcing necrotic cell death can destroy even Oblivion
- **Inflammatory Response:** Each failed apoptotic attempt triggers inflammatory cytokine release, potentially alerting broader immune response
- **The Weight of Unending:** Philosophical vulnerability; Oblivion has not experienced death in so long that it has forgotten the meaning of life

## Narrative Significance

### The Mirror of Chrono-01
Oblivion and Chrono-01 (Telomerase-Positive Tumor Stem Cell) are often considered twin pillars of Neoplasm immortality—**eternal life** and **deathless existence**. Where Chrono-01 grants the ability to divide forever, Oblivion removes the escape of dying.

> *"Chrono-01 and I are two halves of the same curse. They give life without end. I deny death without reason. Together, we are perfect... and perfectly hollow."*

### The Philosophy of Refusal
Oblivion's worldview is a dark mirror of the Neoplasm cause's broader ideology.
