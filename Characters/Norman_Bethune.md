## Historical Background

### The Making of a Revolutionary Surgeon

Norman Bethune was born in Gravenhurst, Ontario, Canada, in 1890. His grandfather was a renowned surgeon, and Norman followed in his footsteps, graduating from the University of Toronto's medical school in 1916.

**His Medical Legacy:**
- **1917:** Served with Canadian forces in World War I as a field surgeon
- **1920s-1930s:** Became a renowned thoracic surgeon in Canada; invented surgical instruments (Bethune rib shears, Bethune pneumothorax apparatus)
- **1935:** Joined the Communist Party of Canada after visiting the Soviet Union
- **1936-1937:** Led a medical team to Spain during the Spanish Civil War, establishing the world's first **mobile blood transfusion service**

**The Spanish Innovation:**
In Spain, Bethune faced a critical problem: wounded soldiers were dying from blood loss before they could receive transfusions. He developed a mobile blood bank that:
- Could transport blood across a 1,000 km front
- Enrolled over 1,000 donors
- Collected approximately 80 units (10 gallons) of blood
- Performed up to 100 transfusions per day
- **Reduced mortality by 30%**

**The Chinese Mission:**
In 1938, Bethune crossed the Pacific to China, drawn by what he called "the most urgent need"—the war against Japanese fascism. He arrived in Yan'an in March 1938 and immediately requested to be sent to the front lines.

**His Final Mission:**
In China, Bethune:
- Served as medical advisor to the Jin-Cha-Ji Military District
- Established field hospitals and trained medical personnel
- Performed over 1,290 surgeries in less than two years
- In October 1939, while operating in a small temple only 3.5 km from the front lines, he cut his left middle finger
- The wound became infected; he developed septicemia
- **November 12, 1939:** Bethune died at age 49

**Mao Zedong's Tribute:**
On December 21, 1939, Mao Zedong wrote the famous essay "In Memory of Norman Bethune," calling him:
> *"A noble man, a pure man, a moral man, a man liberated from vulgar interests, a man beneficial to the people."*

## In SoMe Universe

### The Scarlet Surgeon

In SoMe's universe, Norman Bethune is remembered as the **Scarlet Surgeon**—the commander who proved that **no distance is too great, no front is too dangerous, and no sacrifice is too much** for the cause of healing.

**The Bethune Doctrine:**
> *"The operating table is my battlefield. The scalpel is my weapon. The enemy is death itself—and death does not distinguish between friend or foe, between near or far. I will cross any ocean, scale any mountain, face any fire—because where there are wounded, I must go."*

### The International Medical Corps

Bethune established the **International Medical Corps (IMC)**—a unit within the Heavenly Army that welcomed foreign volunteers, cross-trained personnel from allied nations, and operated in the most dangerous theaters of operation.

**IMC Principles:**
1. **No Borders:** Medical aid transcends national boundaries
2. **Forward Deployment:** Set up surgical stations as close to the front as possible
3. **Mobile Operations:** Maintain mobility to keep pace with shifting battlefields
4. **Local Training:** Train indigenous medical personnel for long-term sustainability
5. **Blood is Life:** Establish reliable blood supply chains in all theaters

### The Scarlet Uniform

Bethune chose to wear a **scarlet uniform** instead of the standard white of the Heavenly Army. This was controversial but intentional:

**The Scarlet Symbolism:**
- **Red** for blood—the vital fluid he fought to preserve
- **Visibility** to indicate his position at the front lines
- **Warning** to enemies: "The healer is here; spare the wounded"
- **Contrast** to the white of enemies (pathogens wear no color)

**His Famous Words:**
> *"The closer to the front, the lower the mortality rate. If soldiers are not afraid of danger on the battlefield, why should we be afraid in the operating room?"*

## Bethune's Contributions to SoMe

### 1. Mobile Blood Transfusion Service (星际流动血库)

Bethune invented the **Mobile Blood Transfusion Service**—a system that could:
- Collect blood from donors in the field
- Store and transport blood across vast distances
- Match blood types quickly
- Administer transfusions at or near the front lines

**The Bethune Blood Bank Protocol:**
```
1. Register all combat personnel for blood type
2. Mark blood types on uniforms and stretcher teams
3. Maintain a pool of universal donors (O negative)
4. Transport blood in portable containers
5. Administer within hours of collection
6. Track all transfusions for post-battle records
```

**In-Game Effect:**
Bethune's blood transfusion technology reduces battlefield mortality by 30% for any unit under his command.

### 2. Forward Surgical Deployment (前沿外科部署)

Bethune pioneered the concept of **forward surgical deployment**—placing surgical teams within 5 km of the front lines, in makeshift facilities (temples, barns, caves).

**The Bethune Standard:**
- **Distance:** Within 5 km of the front line
- **Facility:** Any available structure (temple, barn, cave)
- **Equipment:** Portable surgical kits ("卢沟桥" — Lugou Bridge — named to commemorate the anti-fascist struggle)
- **Endurance:** Continuous operations for 48-72 hours during heavy fighting
- **Priority:** Save lives, then evacuate for definitive care

**In-Game Effect:**
Bethune's forward deployment doctrine allows surgical units to function in any terrain, including hostile environments normally inaccessible to medical personnel.

### 3. Medical Training Revolution (医疗培训革命)

Bethune established **barefoot doctor programs**—rapid training courses that turned ordinary soldiers and civilians into competent medical aides.

**The Bethune Training Protocol:**
- **Duration:** 6-12 months
- **Focus:** Battlefield first aid, wound treatment, evacuation procedures
- **Philosophy:** "Every soldier is a potential medic; every medic is a potential surgeon"
- **Legacy:** Trained thousands of medical personnel who continued the fight long after Bethune's death

**In-Game Effect:**
Bethune's training programs increase the medical capacity of allied units by 50%.

### 4. Instrument Innovation (器械创新)

Bethune invented numerous surgical instruments that remain in use today:
- **Bethune Rib Shears:** Heavy-duty rib cutters for thoracic surgery
- **Bethune Pneumothorax Apparatus:** Device for collapsing lungs during surgery
- **The Lugou Bridge Pack:** Portable surgical kit containing 100 operations' worth of supplies

**In-Game Effect:**
Bethune's instruments grant +20% surgical success rate and reduce operation time by 25%.

During the Japanese winter "mopping-up" campaign, Bethune refused to evacuate. He set up his surgical station in Sunjia Village temple, only 3.5 km from the front. The battle was fierce; shells fell near his operating table. It was during this operation that his left middle finger was cut.

## The Final Sacrifice

### The Wound (October 1939)

During a routine surgery, Bethune's left middle finger was punctured by a scalpel. The wound was minor—but the battlefield conditions were not.

**The Infection:**
- Bacteria from the wound entered his bloodstream
- Septicemia developed
- His condition deteriorated rapidly
- Despite rest and treatment, the infection spread

### The Final Letter

On November 11, 1939, Bethune dictated his last letter to Commander Nie Rongzhen:

> *"Dear Commander Nie, Today I feel very ill—perhaps I will say farewell to you forever. Please inform the Communist Parties of Canada and the United States that I am very happy here. My only wish is to contribute more. Tell them to buy 250 pounds of quinine and 300 pounds of iron supplements each year, for the treatment of malaria and anemia patients. Do not buy from Baoding or the Beijing-Tianjin area—they are twice as expensive as Shanghai or Hong Kong..."*

**His Final Words:**
> *"I am here very happily. The last two years have been the happiest and most meaningful days of my life."*

### The Ascension (November 12, 1939)

At dawn on November 12, 1939, in Huangshikou Village, Hebei, Norman Bethune breathed his last. He was 49 years old.

**In SoMe Lore:**
Bethune's consciousness did not dissipate. His final sacrifice—giving his life while treating others—elevated him to a **legendary status** within the Heavenly Army. He became a patron saint of battlefield medicine, internationalism, and the principle that **no distance is too great, no danger is too great, no sacrifice is too much for the cause of healing**.
